# Sauti Adapt visual direction

**Status:** Working creative direction, November 2026. This is a fictional design council exercise, not a real conversation with the named designers or proof of user testing.

## Imagined creative review

**Steve Jobs, imagined creative chair:** Make the primary action unmistakable. When the user arrives, they should recognize that Sauti learns their voice rather than demanding that they learn technical settings.

**Jony Ive, imagined visual advisor:** Use depth, light and careful typography. Reserve spectacle for the welcome moment; keep the recording interface quiet.

**Don Norman, imagined interaction advisor:** Provide clear system state, obvious recovery, visible progress and excellent feedback for every action.

**Kat Holmes, imagined inclusive design advisor:** Motor abilities and speech patterns vary. Accommodate pauses, retries, preferred recording controls, large targets and different input methods.

**Hypothetical participant perspective:** Don't assume all people with cerebral palsy have the same dexterity, speech, fatigue or accessibility preferences. Listen to actual participants before declaring the product accessible.

## Rewritten implementation prompt

Design and implement a premium, human centered Sauti Adapt user experience inspired by:
1. The luminous violet atmosphere and elegant panels of the supplied music interface.
2. The sophisticated hierarchy and dark service dashboard of the supplied cardiology interface.
3. The immersive, confident editorial treatment of the supplied landscape travel interface.

Do not reproduce the supplied screenshots or add unrelated medical imagery. Give the participant an atmospheric but readable welcome, a single obvious next action, large recording buttons, comfortable spacing, explicit feedback, a visible replay and retry path, and optional technical disclosures. Maintain adjustable recording time and spoken guidance. Give the public research dashboard a separate dark visual style without displaying participant records or fabricating metrics. Respect motion preferences and keyboard focus. Keep all backend privacy, consent and authentication protections unchanged. Test mobile layout, the existing onboarding and recording journey, and live deployment.

## Visual decisions

* **Participant palette:** deep midnight violet for the welcome, warm white for content, and concentrated electric violet for the primary action.
* **Research palette:** dark navy, muted amethyst, bright readable type and small truthful system status surfaces.
* **Distinctive element:** a purely decorative CSS voice sphere and waveform, not a video or a recording indicator.
* **Primary content:** generous typography, readable labels, restrained rounded surfaces and controls at least 54px high where possible.
* **Reduced effort:** progress visible at every stage, technical training content collapsed by default, no decorative animations.
* **Truthfulness:** a service reporting ready does not establish recognition accuracy or participant readiness.

## Validation obligations

Passing automated tests and observing clean responsive layout do not substitute for usability sessions with people who have cerebral palsy. Check one hand operation, assistive switches, keyboard navigation, speech fatigue, optional recording time limits, accidental taps, clarity of consent, confidence under microphone errors and screen reader feedback with real participants.
